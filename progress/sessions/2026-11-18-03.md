## Checkpoint: one probability versus several

- The learner asked to start the study. The related B6 review is due on 2026-11-19 and remains in the queue.
- Introduced a probability distribution as a list of possible outcomes and their probabilities. Exactly one outcome occurs in each example, so the probabilities sum to 1.
- Bernoulli example: a support request is urgent (label `1`) with probability `p=0.7` or not urgent (label `0`) with probability `1-p=0.3`. The scalar `p` determines both probabilities.
- Categorical example: one request goes to exactly one team: billing (`0.7`), technical support (`0.2`), or account support (`0.1`). Knowing the billing probability leaves `0.3` in total for the other two teams but does not determine the split. A categorical distribution with two outcomes can represent the same probabilities as a Bernoulli distribution.

## Checkpoint: MLE at a boundary

## Checkpoint: why the derivative has a negative sign

- Learner asked why `d/dp ln(1-p)` has a negative sign while `d/dp ln(p)` does not.
- Explained the chain rule with outer function `ln(u)` and inner function `u(p)`: `d/dp ln(u(p)) = (1/u) * du/dp`. For `u=p`, `du/dp=1`; for `u=1-p`, `du/dp=-1`. Therefore `d/dp ln(1-p)=(1/(1-p))(-1)=-1/(1-p)`.
- Added the directional intuition: as `p` increases, `1-p` decreases, so `ln(1-p)` also decreases. The chain-rule sign is not yet independently checked on a new inner function.
